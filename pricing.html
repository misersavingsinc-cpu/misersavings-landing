Pricing

You pay when you save. Not before.

One fee, charged only when money moves. Here is what it costs, what it pays for, and when Miser isn't worth it.

$2.50 + 1.5%

per transfer from your checking to your savings. No cap.

  • No subscription. No account fee. No monthly minimum.
  • Nothing moves, nothing is charged.
  • Everything else in the app is free.

What a transfer actually costs

The fee is added on top, so the full amount you skipped lands in your savings. Your checking is debited the amount plus the fee.

$50 (minimum)$3.25$53.256.5%

The flat $2.50 is most of the fee on a small transfer, so the share drops as the transfer grows. The same fee applies to a scheduled transfer and to Add money.

Over a year

Your skips move once at least $50 is locked and 7 days have passed since your last transfer. So what you pay over a year depends on how much you skip in a typical week.

Assumes one transfer a week for 52 weeks. Skip less than $50 in a week and nothing moves: it rolls into the next transfer, and you pay one fee when it does.

What the fee pays for

Every transfer runs over the ACH network through our regulated payment partner, which charges us for each one. The rest keeps Miser running: the app, the coach, support.

The fee is the only way Miser is paid. We don't run ads, we don't sell your data, and we don't earn interest on your savings. Your savings sit in your own account at your own bank, and any interest they earn is your bank's to pay you.

We're a small team, and this fee is where we start, not where we want to end up. Most of it goes straight to the cost of running each transfer, and those costs come down as more people use Miser. Lowering the fee is a goal we're working toward. If it changes, you'll hear it from us before it applies, and the ACH authorization you accept will show the new amount.

How it compares

Your bank's savings rules

Free. Moves a fixed amount on a schedule or rounds up purchases. Saves money, but doesn't change what you do with it.

Subscription savings apps

Usually a few dollars a month. Charged whether you save that month or not.

Miser

$0 in a week you don't save. A fee only on money you chose not to spend, as it moves into your own savings.

A bank rule moves money. Miser builds a habit.

A savings rule moves the same amount every week, whatever you did with the rest. It never touches the moment that matters: the one where you're about to spend.

Miser works at that moment. You catch the impulse, skip it, and watch it go toward something you actually want: a trip, an emergency fund, a deposit on a place of your own. Every skip counts toward a streak and a goal you can see filling. Quitting something that costs you money? The Quit Companion turns every clean day into dollars.

The habits worth breaking are the ones you lose money on. A pack of cigarettes a day at $9 is $3,285 a year. Four drinks a week at $8 is $1,664. Quit, and the Quit Companion puts that money toward your goal one clean day at a time. The takeout, the late-night cart, the drink you didn't need: money that was going to be spent. If Miser turns $50 of it into savings for a goal, you paid $3.25 to keep $50 that was never going to be saved at all.

When Miser isn't worth it

If you already save automatically and rarely spend on impulse, you don't have a habit to fix. Use your bank's free savings rules. They cost nothing and you don't need us.

Straight answers

How much does Miser cost?

Miser is free to download and free to use. When money moves from your checking to your savings, a service fee of $2.50 + 1.5% of that transfer applies, with no cap. A $50 transfer costs $3.25. A $200 transfer costs $5.50. There is no subscription and no account fee.

Is Miser expensive?

It depends on how much you move at once. At the $50 minimum the fee is 6.5% of what you save. At $200 it is 2.75%, and at the $490 maximum it is about 2%. A bank's free savings rule is cheaper if all you want is to move money. Miser is built to change a habit: it catches money you were about to spend and puts it toward a goal you chose.

Do I pay anything in a week I don't save?

No. If nothing moves, nothing is charged. Under $50 locked, no transfer starts and no fee applies; the amount rolls into your next transfer. A subscription app charges you in a month you save nothing. Miser doesn't.

Why does Miser charge per transfer instead of a monthly fee?

Because our cost is per transfer. Every transfer runs over the ACH network through our regulated payment partner, which charges us for each one. Charging per transfer means you only pay in weeks money actually moves. Miser has no ads, does not sell your data, and does not earn interest on your savings, so the fee is how Miser is paid.

Does it cost anything to get my money out?

No. Your savings are in your own savings account at your own bank. You move money out through your bank, the way you always have. Miser charges nothing for that and is not involved.
